Predicted vs measured: where the L1 model stands

—

Every row replays an independently published measurement through the L1 core in two operating points: spec (100% of datasheet peaks) and realistic (compute 70% · DRAM 75% of spec). Signed error = (predicted − measured) / measured; positive means the simulator over-predicts. Full records, URLs, and caveats live in simulator/validation_data.py and the generated validation report.

These are engine/scheduler-level results whose concurrency or per-step overheads are not published, so L1 cannot replay them uniquely. They are recorded here as ground truth for the later L2/L3 stages and to quantify what the roofline ceiling does not cover.

Reading the residuals

Realistic mode lands within ~1–3% on the two single-request H100 Llama-3.1-8B rows and within ~14% on the concurrency-8 end-to-end run, because those workloads are close to the pure DRAM-streaming or prefill-GEMM regimes the L1 model describes. The remaining errors decompose into:

  • Per-step software overhead (kernel dispatch, sampler, Python/API round-trip) — visible as the measured ITL intercept in the vLLM fits;
  • MoE routing/communication latency — GLM-5.3-Flash at batch 1 streams only 18B active bytes (~0.9 ms of DRAM time) but each token pays ~45 layers of sequential expert all-to-all;
  • Backend maturity — the B200/FlashInfer row implies only ~51% of HBM, well under the H100 rows' ~73–75%;
  • Scheduler behavior — MLPerf offline/server rows batch and overlap prefill/decode at unpublished concurrency.
